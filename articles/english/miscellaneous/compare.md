# Rules & Laws


Rules (Quy tắc)

• Scope: Narrow and localized, set by individuals, families, schools, clubs, or sports teams.
• Enforcement: Applied only to members of that specific group with localized consequences (such as losing a turn or getting detention).
• Legal Character: Lacks formal state-backed normative authority.

Laws (Luật)

• Scope: Broad and universal, applying to all people within a state or jurisdiction at all times.
• Enforcement: Created and enacted by official legislative bodies (such as parliaments) and enforced by police and independent courts.
• Legal Character: Carries serious legal consequences for violations, such as heavy fines, property seizure, or imprisonment.

The Rule of Law vs. Rule by Law

• Rule of Law: A political and legal principle stating that no one is above the law—including government officials, sovereign rulers, and institutions—and that laws must be clear, public, stable, and independently adjudicated to protect human rights.
• Rule by Law: A method of governance where authorities place themselves above the law, using legal instruments merely as a tool to control the population, shape public behavior, and enforce compliance.
