- Starting at approximately 200, the number climbed to 500 by the end of the period.
- Having reached a peak in 2010, the figure declined considerably thereafter.

## 10.2. While / Whereas / Although

- While A experienced steady growth, B remained relatively stable.
- Whereas the figure for A increased substantially, that for B declined.
- Although A initially recorded the highest figure, it was eventually overtaken by B.
- While the two categories followed similar patterns initially, their trajectories diverged considerably thereafter.

## 10.3. Relative clauses

- A, which accounted for approximately 30% of the total, was the second-largest category.
- The figure reached 500 in 2015, which represented a substantial increase from the previous year.
- B, which had initially lagged behind A, eventually surpassed it.

## 10.4. With-constructions

- With the figure standing at 500, A remained the largest category.
- With both categories experiencing steady growth, the overall total increased considerably.
- With a difference of only 5%, the two categories recorded broadly comparable proportions.

## 10.5. Comparison structures

- The figure for A was almost twice that of B.
- A recorded a substantially greater proportion than B.

# 12. Expressions to Use Carefully

| Expression | Important distinction |
|---|---|
| increase by | Indicates the magnitude of change |
| increase to | Indicates the final value |
| account for | Describes a proportion of a whole |
| amount to | Describes a total or resulting quantity |
| comprise | Describes what a whole consists of |
| constitute | Describes what makes up a whole |
| exceed | Indicates a higher value |
| outnumber | Specifically compares numbers of people or things |
| overtake | Indicates surpassing another figure or category |
| peak at | Indicates the highest point |
| reach a peak of | Indicates the value at the highest point |
| level off | Indicates stabilisation after a change |
| plateau | Indicates a period or state of stability |
| fluctuate | Indicates repeated variation |
| remain unchanged | Indicates no observable change |
| respectively | Matches items to values in the same order |

## Expressions that are often overused

Avoid relying excessively on:
- a dramatic increase
- a significant decrease
- it can be seen that
- as can be seen from the graph
- the number of
- there was a rise in
- on the other hand
- in conclusion

Prefer more precise alternatives where appropriate.

# 14. Principles for Using Task 1 Vocabulary

1. **Prioritise accuracy over lexical complexity.** A precise verb is more valuable than an unnecessarily sophisticated synonym.
2. **Avoid synonym substitution without considering context.** "Soar", "climb", and "increase" are not always interchangeable in terms of intensity.
3. **Vary grammatical structures, not merely vocabulary.** Combine simple comparisons, subordinate clauses, participial clauses, and passive constructions.
4. **Avoid subjective interpretations.** Task 1 describes observable information; it does not require speculation about causes or implications.
5. **Group related data.** Identify patterns and contrasts instead of describing every figure independently.
6. **Use the overview strategically.** Highlight the most prominent features rather than reproducing individual statistics.
7. **Maintain tense consistency.** Use past simple for completed historical periods, present simple for general descriptions and timeless diagrams, and appropriate future forms for projections.
8. **Do not force advanced vocabulary.** Natural collocations and accurate data descriptions are more important than using rare words.
