Core Document Principles

• Subject-verb agreement: Pair singular subjects with singular verbs (the report outlines) and plural subjects with plural verbs (the reports outline). Ignore intervening phrases (e.g., The list of items is ready).
• Consistency in structure: Keep parallel lists, headings, and bullet points consistently singular or plural across a section.
• Countable vs. uncountable nouns: Do not pluralize uncountable nouns like information, advice, equipment, or software unless used in a specific countable sense.
• Articles and determiners: Use a or an only with singular countable nouns, and match demonstratives (this/that vs. these/those) to noun number.
• Style guide alignment: Choose one convention for technical, legal, or academic terms with dual plurals (such as appendices vs. appendixes or indexes vs. indices) and apply it throughout the document.

Pluralization Rules

• Regular nouns: Add -s to most nouns (document → documents). Add -es to nouns ending in -s, -ss, -sh, -ch, -x, or -z (box → boxes, match → matches).
• Nouns ending in -y: Change -y to -ies after a consonant (policy → policies), but add -s after a vowel (key → keys).
• Nouns ending in -f or -fe: Many change to -ves (leaf → leaves), though exceptions add -s (roof → roofs).
• Nouns ending in -o: Some take -es (hero → heroes) and others take -s (photo → photos, video → videos)—verify in a dictionary or style guide.
• Irregular and invariable nouns: Memorize irregular shifts (child → children, man → men) and unchanged forms (sheep, series, species).
